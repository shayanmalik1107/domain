Subdomains vs Subdirectories: SEO & Architecture Guide

August 24, 2026 • 5 min read

Structuring your web application correctly has a massive impact on search engine crawl budget, domain authority consolidation, and web development complexity.

1. What is a Subdirectory? (`example.com/blog`)

Subdirectories store content within folders under your root domain. Search engines view all subdirectories as part of the primary domain, meaning backlinks and authority flow directly across all pages.

2. What is a Subdomain? (`blog.example.com`)

Subdomains create distinct structural partitions. Google treats subdomains as separate entities for indexing and ranking purposes, which can dilute domain authority across properties.

3. When to Use Subdirectories

For content marketing, blogs, product documentation, and core marketing pages, subdirectories (`example.com/blog`) are strongly recommended by SEO experts because they consolidate link equity.

4. When Subdomains Make Sense

Subdomains (`app.example.com`, `api.example.com`, `shop.example.com`) are ideal when hosting entirely different tech stacks, customer portals, multi-tenant SaaS dashboards, or localized international branches.
