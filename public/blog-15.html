Web3 & Decentralized Domains: ENS vs Traditional DNS

August 24, 2026 • 5 min read

The domain ecosystem is undergoing an architectural shift with the rise of blockchain-based decentralized naming services like the Ethereum Name Service (ENS).

1. Traditional DNS Architecture

Traditional DNS is managed through centralized registries (Verisign, ICANN) and resolved via hierarchical root servers. While highly efficient, it relies on centralized authority control.

2. How Decentralized Domains Work (.eth, .crypto)

Decentralized domains operate as smart contracts and NFTs (Non-Fungible Tokens) stored directly on public blockchains like Ethereum or Polygon. Once minted, ownership is held securely in the user's private crypto wallet.

3. Key Differences

  • Ownership: DNS domains are leased annually; Web3 domains are owned permanently once minted (depending on protocol parameters).
  • Censorship Resistance: Blockchain records cannot be altered or shut down by single centralized entities.
  • Functionality: ENS domains double as crypto wallet payment addresses, decentralized website pointers (IPFS), and Web3 login IDs.

4. Interoperability & The Hybrid Future

Initiatives like ENS DNS integration allow users to link traditional DNS domains (`example.com`) directly to blockchain wallets, bridging Web2 and Web3 ecosystems.
