How to Protect Your Brand from Cybersquatting

August 24, 2026 • 5 min read

Cybersquatting occurs when bad actors register domain names containing established trademarks to profit from brand confusion, extortion, or phishing scams.

1. Register Common Typos & Alternate TLDs

Defensive domain registration is your first line of defense. Register common misspellings (e.g. `googel.com`) and key top-level domains (.com, .ai, .co, .net) before bad actors do.

2. Trademark Protection & Trademark Clearinghouse (TMCH)

Registering your brand name with government trademark offices (USPTO, WIPO) grants legal protection under ICANN policies and enables priority access during new TLD sunrise periods.

3. UDRP (Uniform Domain-Name Dispute-Resolution Policy)

If a cybersquatter registers a domain matching your trademark in bad faith, you can file a UDRP proceeding with WIPO or FORUM to recover the domain without costly court litigation.

4. Continuous WHOIS Monitoring

Use automated WHOIS monitoring tools to receive alerts whenever new domain registrations containing your brand keywords occur across global registries.
